You're actually better positioned than "encoder only" — quick factual correction because it changes the roadmap in your favour: your summariser is
  decoder-only, your classifier is encoder-only, and your original transformer was full encoder-decoder with cross-attention. So you already have all three
  pieces built and gradient-checked in your lib: MLEncoder, MLDecoder, and MLMHA with is_cross_attention (plus the gradient_kv → encoder channel from the
  original seq2seq). Encoder-decoder is a reassembly of tested components, not a new architecture. That's the biggest fact here.

  Concrete steps, here to ~300M:

  1. Reassemble the encoder-decoder. Wire it as: encoder stack over the thread → decoder stack where each block is masked-self-attn + cross-attn(to encoder
  output) + FFN. Every part exists; you're connecting MLEncoder's output into the decoder's cross-attention MLMHA. The gradient channel back to the encoder
  (gradient_kv) is already there from the Perl-era seq2seq.

  2. Gradient-check the assembled model. Run your fp64 finite-diff gate on it, focusing the cross-attention path — test_mha already covers cross-attention,
  so extend it to the full enc-dec. Confirm green before scaling. This is the "assemble tested parts, verify the seam" move you've done all project.

  3. Size to ~300M. Concrete config: d=1024, 10 encoder + 10 decoder layers, 16 heads (head_dim 64), d_ff=4096 → ~300M. (d=768 / 12+12 gives ~200M if you
  want a leaner first cut.) Vocab is now a free choice — the tied-head starvation that forced 8k at 7M is gone (at 300M an 8k vocab is ~2–3% of params, not
  56%), so keep 8k or go to 16k/32k for less literal fragmentation, your call.

  4. Long context to 3000 tokens. FlashAttention on the encoder self-attention (that's the O(seq²) cost at 3000, and you said the coding's fine). The
  decoder is short (~500 out) so cheap; cross-attention is 500×3000, cheaper than the encoder's 3000×3000. Positions need to reach 3000 — your sinusoidal
  encoding extends arbitrarily, or switch to RoPE for better length behaviour (the modern default).

  5. Rebuild the data for threads. Flip the trimmer: keep the thread instead of stripping quoted history, convert HTML→markdown (preserves the > quote
  nesting so the model can tell who-said-what), and re-run the teacher on whole threads to generate thread→summary pairs. This restores teacher==student
  cleanly — the 300M encoder sees the whole 3000-token thread, same as the teacher did.

  6. Pretrain with a seq2seq denoising objective (this is the one genuinely new component — decoder-only next-token doesn't pretrain an encoder-decoder).
  Options, all documented and proven:
  - BART text-infilling — mask spans of the input, reconstruct the original. Simplest strong choice.
  - PEGASUS gap-sentence-generation — mask whole important sentences, generate them. This one is purpose-built for summarisation and is why PEGASUS-large
    (568M) set SOTA on 12 summarisation datasets — the closest-fit objective to your goal.
       Run it on your cosmo + Enron corpus (which you're already encoding).

  7. Fine-tune on the thread→summary pairs from step 5. Standard seq2seq fine-tuning.

  What carries straight over: the bf16 storage work matters more here (the 3000-token encoder attention buffers are the biggest tensors in the whole model,
  so bf16 + FlashAttention there is high-value); your tokenizer, encode/dedup harness, and the cosmo/Enron pretrain corpus all reuse as-is. And
